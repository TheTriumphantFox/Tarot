# The Thorn Reliquary Tarot

## Core identity

The Thorn Reliquary Tarot is an original 78-card deck imagined as a set of
devotional and divinatory leaves assembled between the ninth and eleventh
centuries, rebound in iron, and handled for generations. Its visual language
combines Insular manuscript ornament, early Romanesque figures, primitive
woodcut pressure, egg-tempera color, iron-gall ink, and restrained gold leaf.

The deck should feel mysterious and spiritually serious, but never like a
modern horror prop. Age appears through use: softened corners, hand-oil patina,
smoke, shallow folds, worm tracks, abraded pigment, and flaking gilding. The
symbolic image must always remain readable beneath the wear.

## Card construction

- Portrait ratio: 2:3.
- Every face uses the same narrow iron-reliquary border, four corner rivets,
  braided knotwork, and top circular numeral medallion.
- Major Arcana use Roman numerals only. The Fool uses `0`.
- Numbered Minor Arcana use Arabic numerals only (`2` through `10`); Aces
  use `A`, and courts use `P`, `N`, `Q`, and `K`.
- Avoid long printed titles on the artwork. The application supplies the card
  name separately and the image should resemble a manuscript leaf rather than
  a labeled modern deck.
- Figures use flat medieval perspective, solemn expressive faces, elongated
  hands, symbolic scale, and imperfect hand-drawn geometry.
- Small marginal animals, plants, ruins, tools, and celestial signs carry
  secondary meanings without overcrowding the central subject.

## Palette

| Color | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Soot black | Ink, night fields, deep negative space |
| Smoked umber | Wood, robes, aged outlines |
| Old bone | Vellum, moonlight, pale figures |
| Iron gray | Borders, weapons, winter architecture |
| Oxidized verdigris | Vines, water, healing and decay |
| Faded madder red | Cloth, bloodline, fire, warning |
| Muted ochre | Earth, grain, candlelight |
| Tarnished gold | Halos, celestial marks, rare focal accents |

Gold is a scarce compositional signal, not a general highlight. No neon color,
clean digital gradient, glossy lamination, or modern type belongs in the deck.

## The card back

The back is a non-directional thorn labyrinth held inside the iron border. Four
birds, braided knotwork, and opposed flowers surround a central eclipse wheel.
The wheel uses alternating solar rays and lunar crescents around a small black
star. It contains no faces or upright symbols and remains visually unchanged
after a 180-degree rotation, preventing the back from revealing reversal.

## Minor Arcana suit language

### Wands — hawthorn and ember

Wands are cut hawthorn staves, pilgrim rods, torches, and flowering branches.
Their marginal world contains foxes, salamanders, bees, sparks, hill forts,
summer storms, and red cloth. The suit represents will, movement, appetite,
craft, and conflict. Its dominant accents are madder red, charcoal, and ochre.

### Cups — wells and hammered vessels

Cups are hammered communion vessels, wooden bowls, springs, tidal pools, and
rain catchers. Their margins contain fish, swans, seals, lilies, wells, boats,
and reflected stars. The suit represents feeling, relationship, memory, dream,
and receptivity. Its dominant accents are verdigris, old bone, and tarnished
silver-gray.

### Swords — iron and winter truth

Swords are early medieval seaxes, pattern-welded blades, broken iron, and
ceremonial judgment weapons rather than later rapiers. Ravens, geese, bare
branches, wind, towers, and narrow bridges appear around them. The suit
represents thought, language, truth, anxiety, separation, and decision. Its
dominant accents are iron gray, soot black, and cold bone.

### Pentacles — coins, seals, and the worked earth

Pentacles are stamped pilgrimage coins and carved roundels bearing a simple
five-petal star, never modern occult discs. Their world contains grain, cattle,
orchards, stone walls, smithies, looms, scales, and burial hoards. The suit
represents resources, labor, body, home, inheritance, and stewardship. Its
dominant accents are ochre, umber, verdigris, and muted gold.

## Number cards

Ace through Ten use illuminated emblem arrangements rather than repeating the
same cinematic scene. Pip count remains immediately legible, while a small
marginal narrative changes the meaning: sprouting, exchanging, defending,
breaking, overflowing, binding, harvesting, or abandoning. Upright composition
should still feel intentional when a card is displayed reversed; wear and
asymmetry belong to the painted leaf, not the card back.

## Court cards

- Page: novice, messenger, apprentice, or young pilgrim encountering the suit.
- Knight: mounted retainer, traveler, or sworn bearer actively pursuing it.
- Queen: abbess, household matriarch, healer, craft mistress, or keeper who
  embodies its inward and relational authority.
- King: steward, judge, war leader, master smith, or landholder who embodies its
  outward responsibility and potential misuse.

Courts should show varied ages, appearances, and social roles while remaining
plausible inhabitants of the same early-medieval symbolic world.

## Major Arcana rule

Each Major is a singular manuscript vision rather than a copy of Rider-Waite
staging. Preserve the card's archetypal function, then express it through the
deck's world: ruined Roman roads, monastic workshops, plague bells, wells,
harvests, eclipses, winter fields, hill forts, reliquaries, and pilgrimage.
Death remains transformative rather than gory; the Devil concerns bondage and
bargain rather than spectacle; the Tower is failed human certainty rather than
an action scene.

## Wear consistency

All cards share the same age, but not identical damage. Keep the border and
central symbol intact. Vary only small secondary wear marks: one softened
corner, a faint water tide, several pinholes, a shallow fold, smoke at one edge,
or scattered pigment loss. Avoid dramatic tears, burned-away content, fake
coffee rings, and uniformly applied grunge overlays.

## Production constraints

- Generate each card individually at the same portrait dimensions.
- Keep the complete card inside frame with a small plain margin for cropping.
- Reuse the established border, numeral medallion, palette, materials, and wear.
- No captions, logos, watermarks, photorealistic people, Renaissance realism,
  Victorian occult styling, modern fantasy armor, or explicit gore.
- Validate numeral style and visible pip counts after every generation. Every
  numbered Minor must make each suit object continuously traceable at card
  size; ambiguous overlaps count as a production failure even when the
  intended total is correct.
- Preserve a clean master before any application-specific crop or resize.
